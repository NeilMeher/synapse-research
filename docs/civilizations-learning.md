# GAME OF CIVILIZATIONS learning guide

## Two goods and one labor constraint

With two hours, agent A produces 4 units of A or 2 units of B in each hour. It gives up 1/2 B for one extra A. Agent B produces 2 A or 4 B per hour and gives up 2 B per A. Agent A has comparative advantage in A and agent B in B. Each can consume [4,2] or [2,4] after dividing labor between both goods. If they specialize and exchange four units of A for four B, each receives a bundle of [4,4]. Both satisfy two additional units of demand given need [4,4].

## Reconcile flows

Total stored = initial + produced − consumed − explicit losses. Internal transfers disappear from the total because one account's debit is another's credit. When a payer gives A but receives no B, the payer loses A and the supplier gains A. The exchange can change who eats while preserving totals. A cap can destroy stock explicitly after consumption, so all such units appear in the loss column.

## Practice with the world map

Load the same climate and seed in No exchange and Fixed prior. Move to day 24 and compare storage losses, intake and the sum of deliveries. In pooled history inspect a rejected offer and recover the threshold from the payer's expected gain. 

**Solution.** At quantity q and delivery belief p, expected gain is q(3p−2). Acceptance is strictly better than refusal when p exceeds 2/3. Under the policy, the exact tie is sampled fairly. A more selective payer can avoid some non-deliveries while making fewer trades. The aggregate effect depends on how the changed offers affect both sides over time.

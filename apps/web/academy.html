    Probability, correlation and higher-order dependence

    Prerequisites Fractions, averages and the idea of an event.

    Beginner · Individual accuracy and collective accuracy

    If a voter is right with probability q, a panel of three independent voters is right by majority when exactly two or all three are correct. For q=0.65, its accuracy is 3(0.65²)(0.35)+0.65³=0.71825. If the three always share the same report the panel accuracy is 0.65.

    Intermediate · Moments of the count

    Write Xᵢ=1 for a correct report and let K=∑Xᵢ. Under exchangeability, q=E[Xᵢ]=E[K]/n. Expand K(K−1) into a sum over ordered pairs. There are n(n−1) terms, each with expectation E[XᵢXⱼ]. Thus E[XᵢXⱼ]=E[K(K−1)]/[n(n−1)] and ρ=(E[XᵢXⱼ]−q²)/[q(1−q)]. Many count distributions share these two moments. Majority accuracy sums the mass where K>n/2.

    Advanced · Triple agreement

    Three pairwise-independent fair bits can share a parity constraint. Let X₃ be the exclusive-or of independent fair X₁ and X₂. The four equally likely outcomes are 000, 011, 101 and 110. Each pair has the same joint law as independent fair bits. Majority one occurs with probability 3/4, versus 1/2 for three mutually independent fair bits.

    Practice and worked solution

    Change q in the independent three-voter example to 0.8. What fraction of majorities are right? When all three copy one report, what fraction are right?

    Exactly two correct contributes 3(0.8²)(0.2)=0.384. Three correct contributes 0.8³=0.512. The independent total is 0.896. Under copying all voters are correct together with probability 0.8.

    Bayesian inference and confidence calibration

    Prerequisites Conditional probability and percentages.

    Beginner · An agreement changes confidence

    Your answer is right with prior probability a. An independent hint is right with probability h under the symmetric binary evidence model. If the hint agrees, the two mechanisms for agreement are both right or both wrong. Their probabilities are ah and (1−a)(1−h). Dividing the both-right term by their sum gives the probability that your answer is correct.

    Intermediate · Update odds and score the forecast

    Bayes' rule gives posterior odds = prior odds × likelihood ratio. With a=0.60 and h=0.75, agreement multiplies the prior odds 1.5 by 3, yielding 4.5/(1+4.5)=0.8182. On disagreement the ratio is 1/3, yielding 0.5/(1+0.5)=0.3333. Calibration asks whether events forecast at p occur a fraction p of the time. The binary Brier score averages (p−y)² for outcomes y∈{0,1}.

    Update and score a probability

    The score uses a fixed teaching group with outcomes 1, 1, 1, 0. MIRAGE's calibration explorer uses separate archived synthetic tasks.

    Advanced · Why the Brier score rewards honest probabilities

    For an event with true probability θ, the expected score is θ(1−p)²+(1−θ)p². Expand it to θ(1−θ)+(p−θ)². The second term reaches zero only at p=θ. A model can produce accurate decisions while assigning unreliable probabilities. Calibration and discrimination measure different properties.

    Practice and worked solution

    Four claims each have confidence 0.8. Outcomes are 1, 1, 0 and 1. Find their empirical event rate and Brier score.

    Three of four occur, so the rate is 0.75. The mean score is [3(0.2²)+0.8²]/4=0.19. The forecast exceeds the observed rate by 0.05 in this small group.

    Financial market accounting and price formation

    Prerequisites Signed numbers, multiplication and a basic idea of buying and selling.

    Beginner · A trade is a set of transfers

    One buyer acquires a share and transfers the execution price to the seller. The fee account receives the fee. For a buy at 102 with a fee of 1, the buyer's cash changes by −103, the market maker's by +102, and the fee account's by +1. Their sum is zero. The buyer's asset change is +1 and the maker's is −1.

    Intermediate · Inventory-based quotes

    In the REFLEX ledger the midpoint is max(s+fee+1, p₀+λ(I₀−I)). The half-spread s gives bid midpoint−s and ask midpoint+s. Start from p₀=100, s=2 and λ=3. The first buy executes at 102. The inventory falls by one, so the midpoint moves to 103. A second identical buy would execute at 105. The transfer totals still reconcile.

    Total cash and asset count are conserved. Total marked wealth is C+AP when every account is marked at common midpoint P.     The gross transfer is 102+105=207. Two fees of 1 raise the buyer's debit to 209. Maker cash rises by 207 and fee cash by 2. Two shares change hands. The midpoint after the second buy is 106.

    Scientific hypotheses, model selection and information gain

    Prerequisites Bayes' rule, logarithms and means.

    Beginner · An observation can distinguish candidate laws

    If two laws make almost the same predictions where you measure, a precise observation can still provide little information about which law generated them. Choose inputs where the measurement is expected to change the model probabilities.

    Intermediate · Prior and expected posterior entropy

    Write H(p)=−∑pₘlog₂pₘ. The experimental information is H(M)−∑ᵧP(y)H(M|y). For two equally likely models and a binary test with P(+|A)=0.8 and P(+|B)=0.2, P(+)=0.5. The posterior weight of A becomes 0.8 after a positive or 0.2 after a negative. Both have entropy 0.7219 bits, so expected information is 0.2781 bits.

    Enumerate an exact two-model experiment

    Advanced · Integrate over the predictive distribution

    For continuous observations replace the sum by an integral over the model-weighted predictive density. That density includes measurement noise and parameter uncertainty. NOESIS's archived acquisition rule computes the weighted variance of predictive means. It is a disagreement score with different mathematics. When models share a mean but have different predictive variances, that score can be zero even when a measurement is informative about the model.

    Practice and worked solution

    If both models predict a positive observation with probability 0.9 and the prior of A is 0.25, what is expected information gain?

    For a positive, P(A|+)=0.25×0.9/0.9=0.25. For a negative the same calculation uses 0.1 and yields 0.25. Neither possible outcome changes the entropy, so information gain is zero.

    Hypothesis testing, multiplicity and false discoveries

    Prerequisites Probability, sorted lists and complements.

    Beginner · A family of tests creates more opportunities for error

    At level 0.05, a valid continuous null p-value falls below the threshold with probability 0.05. For m independent true nulls the probability that every test avoids rejection is (1−0.05)ᵐ. The complement is the family-wise error probability. At m=20 it is about 0.6415.

    Intermediate · Different adjustments target different risks

    Bonferroni divides α by m. The union bound gives probability of any false rejection at most m₀α/m≤α, even with dependent tests. Holm compares ordered p-values to α/(m−i+1) and stops at the first failure. At the first true null, m−i+1 is at least the number m₀ of true nulls, giving the same union-bound argument. BH keeps all ranks up to the last satisfying p₍ᵢ₎≤iα/m. Its guarantee under independent tests concerns E[V/max(R,1)], with V false reports and R total reports.

    Explore exact family risk and report thresholds

    The adjustment is applied separately to the fixed vector [0.005, 0.012, 0.028, 0.140].

    Advanced · Compare error control and power under a common budget

    If a selection stage devotes half of n to screening and half to independent validation, the second-stage standardized signal is μ√(n/2) for known unit variance. The full test uses μ√n. Independent data protect validation from first-stage selection. This alone does not restore power lost from using a smaller validation sample.

    Practice and worked solution

    For [0.005, 0.012, 0.028, 0.140] at α=0.05, which tests pass Holm and BH?

    Holm's first two thresholds are 0.0125 and approximately 0.0167. The third is 0.025, so Holm stops with two rejections. BH's third threshold is 0.0375 and its fourth is 0.05. The fourth fails and the third passes, so BH rejects the first three.

    Game theory and expected utility

    Prerequisites Weighted averages, probability distributions and table coordinates.

    Beginner · Read a payoff cell

    The row and column identify the two players' actions. A cell such as (4, 4) gives the focal player 4 and the opponent 4. A pure strategy selects one action in this game. A mixed strategy assigns probabilities to both actions.

    Intermediate · Best responses and domination

    If the opponent uses probability q for its first action, the first row's utility is q times the first payoff plus (1−q) times the second. The row with higher utility is a best response. A strictly dominant action beats the other row against each pure opponent action and therefore against every mixture. Mutual best responses give a Nash equilibrium.

    Advanced · Relabeling and stochastic responses

    When a column permutation sends each displayed column j to canonical column σ(j), also replace its weight by qσ(j). The weighted sum stays the same by reindexing. Moving rows permutes utilities. Applying the inverse row map then recovers the canonical response. A logit response gives higher utilities larger probability with sensitivity β. The AXIOM calculation stipulates the opponent distribution. A quantal response equilibrium solves responses jointly.

    Practice and worked solution

    At q = 3/4, find the assurance game's best response. Move the first opponent action to the second displayed column while leaving the 3/4 weight on the first displayed column. What changed?

    The initial utilities are 4×(3/4) = 3 and 3. Both actions are best responses. Failing to carry the weight gives the first canonical opponent action probability 1/4 and changes the first row utility to 1. The second row stays at 3. The inverse-mapped distribution changes from (1/2, 1/2) to (0, 1), a total variation of 1/2.

    Comparative advantage and resource accounting

    Prerequisites Ratios, inequalities and addition of signed quantities.

    Beginner · One constraint, many production plans

    With two labor hours, an A specialist can make four units of A or two B each hour. A B specialist can make two units of A or four B. Each feasible bundle comes from allocating both hours across the two activities.

    Intermediate · Opportunity cost and barter

    Agent A trades off 1/2 B per additional A, while agent B forgoes two B for one A. Opportunity cost, the next best output lost, determines comparative advantage. With one hour per activity, their bundles are [4,2] and [2,4]. Specialization produces [8,0] and [0,8]. Transferring four each gives [4,4] to each. Both fill four units total of previously unmet need if each needed four of both goods.

    Transfer a bundle on the exact ledger

    Advanced · Individual and aggregate accounting

    For resource r, sum each participant's inventory equation. Each debit −q has a credit +q, so gross transfers cancel. Only initial stock, output, consumption and explicit loss determine the aggregate. Need satisfaction and distribution can nevertheless change without creating resources.

    Practice and worked solution

    Keep full specialization and exchange two units. Who meets at least as much need as under divided labor?

    A holds [6,2] and B [2,6]. Each consumes six units under the four-per-good cap, exactly their divided-labor outcome. At four transferred units both consume eight. At zero they each consume only four.

    Cooperation and complex systems

    Prerequisites Expected utility, probability, and the resource ledger.

    Beginner · What changes when interactions repeat?

    A funded exchange can complete or default. Past delivery gives subsequent buyers one way to revise a belief about a supplier. Reciprocity uses observations from prior interactions to condition the next action. A reputation system determines whose records are visible. Placement on the ring determines which agents can interact.

    Intermediate · Derive a decision threshold

    Paying two utility units for one exchange unit yields an anticipated gain of 3p−2 if delivery has probability p. The payoff is positive above p=2/3. Start with three prior successes and four prior observations. A failure changes belief from 3/4 to 3/5. A later successful record changes it to 4/6. A fixed-prior rule never uses these records.

    Change access to past records in a matched model world

    The display reruns an educational stress world with the same seed for each policy.

    Advanced · From decisions to an aggregate distribution

    Compute Gini for nonnegative total individual consumption as ΣᵢΣⱼ|cᵢ−cⱼ| divided by 2nΣᵢcᵢ. With [2,4], the numerator is 4 and the denominator is 24, giving 1/6. Equal aggregate intake can hide different distributions. In POLIS the prescribed accept objective changes which exchanges occur and which holdings are available on later days. The rule is imposed. The later distribution follows from its interaction with the economy.

    Practice and worked solution

    After one failed delivery, does the modeled payer with pooled records accept another ordinary offer from the same supplier? Does a payer with no record accept?

    The first payer's belief of 3/5 gives a gain of 3×0.6−2 = −0.2 per unit, so it rejects. The other uses 3/4 and receives 0.25 expected utility per unit, so it accepts. Both calculations use the same physical offer terms.

    Input–output economics

    Prerequisites Fractions, matrices and keeping track of units.

    Beginner · Follow one purchase

    Suppose a construction firm purchases $0.20 of domestic equipment for each dollar of output. A $100 million order for construction then requires $20 million of equipment in the first production round.

    Intermediate · Read the accounts as a system

    Let rows be suppliers and columns be buyers. Then Aᵢⱼ=Zᵢⱼ/xⱼ. With final demand d, production satisfies x=Ax+d. When the domestic rounds converge, x=d+Ad+A²d+⋯=(I−A)⁻¹d. Imported intermediate and value-added coefficients are multiplied by x separately.

    Vary the construction order

    Advanced · Check convergence and aggregation

    For a nonnegative A and a positive vector v, the largest ratio (Av)ᵢ/vᵢ bounds the rate of production rounds in the associated weighted norm. A bound below one proves convergence. Grouping sums the underlying transaction block and gross output before division. Averaging coefficients arbitrarily changes the model.

    Practice and worked solution

    What if equipment buys $0.10 of construction per dollar and the order is $100 million for construction?

    Construction output C=100+0.10E and equipment output E=0.20C. Substitution yields 0.98C=100, so C≈102.04 and E≈20.41. Gross output is approximately 122.45 million. The extra output above direct demand is 22.45 million. Value added requires separate information about intermediate purchases, and gross output includes those purchases.

    Energy and engineering mathematics

    Prerequisites Rates, fractions and multiplying quantities with units.

    Beginner · Watts describe a rate

    Power is the rate of using energy. One kilowatt for one hour uses one kilowatt-hour. One megawatt equals 1,000 kW and one megawatt-hour equals 1,000 kWh. Compare peak kW when sizing an electrical connection and total kWh when estimating energy charges.

    Intermediate · Separate utilization from load factor

    For an IT baseline of 10 kW, an additional 20 kW at full load, and workload utilization u, average IT power is 10+20u. At u=0.5 it is 20 kW. Load factor is average divided by peak, so it is 20/30=2/3. With constant PUE of 1.5, average facility power becomes 30 kW, or 720 kWh in 24 hours.

    Vary the load in a 24-hour engineering model

    Advanced · Different energy and water boundaries

    PUE uses total facility energy divided by IT energy over the same interval. When an example assumes constant PUE, multiply IT kWh by PUE once. For site WUE of 0.5 L/kWh IT, the 480 IT kWh at half load gives 240 liters of site water, or 0.24 m³. Regional water effects need different inputs.

    Practice and worked solution

    Set workload utilization to 0%. Does the facility still use energy? At full load, how much energy does the building use in a day?

    Idle IT still draws 10 kW. At PUE 1.5 the facility uses 15×24=360 kWh. At full load it draws 45 kW and uses 1,080 kWh. Both calculations hold cooling efficiency fixed.

    Infrastructure project finance

    Prerequisites Compound interest, cash flow and percentages.

    Beginner · Compare money across years

    At 10%, receiving $110 next year has a present value of $100 today. The project discounts unlevered cash flows. Equity discounts the flows after borrowing and servicing debt, with its own required rate. IRR is a rate at which the present value sums to zero.

    Intermediate · Work through principal and cash flow

    Build a $100 facility, fund $50 with debt at 10%, and repay $25 of principal in each of two years. The first interest bill is $5 and the second $2.50. Scheduled payments are $30 and $27.50. With CFADS of $60 per year, DSCR is 2.00× followed by 2.18×. Project cash flows are [−100, 60, 60]. Equity flows are [−50, 30, 32.50]. At 10% project NPV is $4.13. At a 15% equity rate, equity NPV is $0.66.

    Change the annual cash available for debt service

    Advanced · Coverage, break even and ambiguous returns

    NPV=ΣCFₜ/(1+r)ᵗ. Break-even cash flow here is $100 divided by the present value of two $1 payments, or $57.62. Separately, year one needs $30 to pay debt before any reserve. Cash-flow series with several changes of sign may have several IRRs. At 10% and 20% the series [−100,230,−132] has zero NPV, so consult the NPV profile.

    Practice and worked solution

    Set annual CFADS to $25. Can the project pay each year without reserves?

    With no reserve, $25 leaves $5 uncovered in year one and $2.50 in year two. Coverage is 0.83× and 0.91×. These arithmetic results do not determine legal insolvency.

    Optimization and uncertainty

    Prerequisites Inequalities, comparative costs and basic probability.

    Beginner · Test feasibility before ranking

    The objective describes what to minimize. A constraint describes what is allowed. If a site has a 100 m³ water limit and a design uses 120 m³, it is infeasible regardless of cost. An option dominates another when it is no worse on every objective and better on at least one.

    Intermediate · Continuous and discrete choices

    For a linear example, let x and y be nonnegative, x+y≤1, and x≤0.6. Maximizing 2x+y means using all the budget, because both coefficients are positive. Along x+y=1 it yields 1+x, so the solution is x=0.6, y=0.4. Discrete facility options use explicit enumeration because a choice between complete cooling designs cannot be treated as divisible without a separate engineering model.

    Advanced · Correlated shocks

    For a sum X+Y, the variance is Var(X)+Var(Y)+2 Cov(X,Y). Suppose two costs are each 0 or 2 with equal probability. When they are independent, total cost is 4 in one quarter of cases. With perfectly paired high and low costs, total cost is 4 in half the cases. Monte Carlo estimates such frequencies under the sampled joint distribution. The registered ENERGIA scenarios give deterministic comparisons without probabilities.

    Practice and worked solution

    Options A and B cost $1.20 and $1.10 per GPU-hour and use 70 and 90 m³. Option C costs $1.25 and uses 110 m³. A project has a 100 m³ water limit. Can any option dominate every feasible competitor?

    C is infeasible. A uses less water and B costs less, so neither dominates the other. Reducing the water ceiling to 80 m³ leaves only A feasible. This describes these three options under their assumptions.

    Historical statistics and economic measurement

    Prerequisites Fractions, growth rates, units and the accounting distinction between balances and transactions.

    Beginner · A measurement at a date versus activity over a period

    Route kilometres refer to the network reported as of March 31. Freight tonnes count activity across a fiscal year, April through March. An observation through November covers a different interval. A count of parallel running tracks describes a different quantity from the length of the underlying route.

    Intermediate · Derive an index and an annualized rate

    For x₀>0, an index of 100xₜ/x₀ gives 100 at the base. If xₙ=x₀(1+g)ⁿ, taking the nth root gives g=(xₙ/x₀)^(1/n)−1. From 53,596 to 69,439 route km over 74 elapsed years, cumulative change was 29.56% and the equivalent annualized rate was 0.35%. Missing intermediate years supply no annual growth observations.

    Causal inference

    Prerequisites Historical measurement, expectations and conditional comparisons.

    Beginner · What would the same unit have experienced?

    Potential outcome Y(1) describes a unit under treatment and Y(0) the same unit without it. The individual effect is Y(1)−Y(0). One world is observed for each unit in a usual observational design. To estimate a mean effect on treated units, we need a credible comparison for those units under no treatment.

    Intermediate · Derive the selection term

    The observed treated–control mean contrast equals E[Y(1)−Y(0)|T=1] plus E[Y(0)|T=1]−E[Y(0)|T=0]. The second term is baseline selection in untreated potential outcomes. A causal diagram can represent a prior factor X with arrows to both T and Y. Adjusting X depends on the assumed graph and a comparable sample.

    Change baseline selection in a synthetic example

    Advanced · Interference and anticipation

    Potential outcomes may depend on a neighbouring unit's treatment as well as one's own. Traffic diversion is an example. If future treatment also changes pre-period behavior, a measured baseline includes anticipation. Ask whether the design identifies treatment on a unit, membership in a network, or another estimand.

    Practice and worked solution

    A post-only treated–control difference is 12. The treated units would average 8 higher without the intervention. What is the ATT in this teaching setup?

    Subtract 8 from the observed 12, giving 4. In a real nonrandomized study that counterfactual 8 needs independent justification.

    Econometric research designs

    Prerequisites Potential outcomes, means, linear equations and sampling uncertainty.

    Beginner · Use both group and time comparisons

    In a panel, each unit appears at multiple dates. A two-period difference-in-differences compares the treated unit's change to the control's change. Group and time fixed effects capture a fixed level gap and a common post effect in the simple two-by-two design.

    Intermediate · Read a regression coefficient from four means

    Write Y=a+bT+cP+δTP, where T=1 for treated and P=1 after treatment. The four means are a, a+b, a+c and a+b+c+δ. Subtracting differences gives δ=(treated post−treated pre)−(control post−control pre). Identifying a treatment effect requires parallel untreated trends and compatible exposure.

    Break the parallel trends assumption

    Advanced · Designs for multiple units and times

    An event study shows changes by relative event date. Staggered adoption and varying effects can invalidate naïve fixed-effects comparisons. Compare appropriate cohorts and eligible untreated units. Synthetic control forms a documented weighted comparison based on pre-period fit. Placebo tests and clustering choices need a defensible assignment model and enough independent units.

    Practice and worked solution

    Control moves 20→23 and treated moves 28→38. The treated untreated trend would be three larger than the control's. Compute DiD and its bias if ATT is 4.

    DiD=(38−28)−(23−20)=7. Bias is 7−4=3, the unequal untreated trend. A confidence interval around the same biased formula would not restore identification.

    Scientific modelling and uncertainty

    Prerequisites Statistical tests, historical measurement and sensitivity analysis.

    Beginner · Different uncertainty calls for different evidence

    Sampling uncertainty varies with repeated data collection. Parameter uncertainty concerns inputs with limited evidence. Measurement uncertainty concerns units, rounding or source provenance. Structural uncertainty concerns whether the equations and boundaries fit the world. External validity concerns transport to another population or setting.

    Intermediate · Test whether rounding can explain a discrepancy

    A number published to a step h has a half-step rounding interval. A reference differs from its rounded counterpart by at most h/2 on that premise. With a display of 69.44 thousand route km, h=10 km. The precise 69,439 km falls within the implied display interval. The comparison cannot resolve a multi-thousand-kilometre discrepancy.

    Change the reported comparison value

    Advanced · Sensitivity and replication

    Track how different plausible input values change an output. A sensitivity grid is conditional on the chosen grid, while repeated sampling can estimate a sampling distribution under a justified model. Independent code validates a calculation. External replication also checks the population, measurements and research design.

    Practice and worked solution

    If two models perfectly reproduce their own simulations and diverge on a new case, which forms of uncertainty matter?

    Check numerical reproducibility first, then compare model structure, parameters and target populations. Matching archived numbers can eliminate one computational explanation while measurement or external validity questions remain open.

    Experimental design and measurement bias

    A choice has a meaning, a printed label and a location. An experiment can change one while holding the others fixed. If A is always the first option, preference for A and preference for position one are observationally coupled.

    Move the correct answer

    This deterministic demonstration uses a four-choice problem with exactly one correct answer and two fixed response rules.

    Counterbalance and pair

    In a four-row Latin square, each content appears in each position once. An independently balanced letter assignment can make each correct content appear under A, B, C and D. MIRAGE 002 adds a separate grammar ordering and changes one presentation factor at a time relative to its reference. Its cue effects compare multiple presentations of the same canonical task. The uncertainty calculation resamples tasks, so sixteen presentations of one question remain one experimental unit.

    Check outcome-dependent missingness

    On four paired problems, suppose observed changes in loss are +0.10, +0.20 and −0.10, and the fourth is missing in both arms. The complete-pair mean is +0.067. Because Brier loss is in [0,1], the missing difference is in [−1,1]. The full-plan average therefore ranges from (0.20−1)/4 to (0.20+1)/4, or −0.20 to +0.30.

    Practice and worked solution

    Give the correct answer label D in the first position. Which rule is correct? What happens if you keep label D and display the correct answer second?

    The first-position rule succeeds in the original arrangement while always-A fails. Moving the answer to position two makes both rules fail. Changing just the label to A would instead make always-A succeed while leaving the position rule unchanged.

    Sampling and response distributions

    A decoder turns token scores into output choices. Greedy decoding takes the best-ranked token. Sampling draws a token from a probability distribution. Temperature rescales score differences before sampling and changes the probabilities of competing strings.

    For a categorical distribution with probabilities pᵢ, Shannon entropy is H=−Σ pᵢ log₂(pᵢ). A fixed response has entropy zero. Four equally probable outputs have entropy 2 bits because each probability is 1/4. Entropy measures uncertainty about the next response category. Correctness needs a separate answer key.

    Vary the probability of one label

    This mathematical demonstration divides the remaining probability equally between B, C and D. The correct answer is fixed at B.

    Measure at the task level

    Five samples from one question give five draws within a task. A treatment interval across questions should resample the whole group of draws for each question. A plug-in entropy estimate from five draws can miss rare answers and is generally low on average. MIRAGE 004 reports the number of observed draws beside its entropy estimates.

    Practice and worked solution

    Committee optimization and correlated errors

    For each question, let Eᵢ equal 1 if member i fails and 0 otherwise. Joint failure P(Eᵢ=1,Eⱼ=1) can exceed P(Eᵢ=1)P(Eⱼ=1). The excess, divided by the product of the indicators’ standard deviations, gives Pearson phi. If either member never varies, phi is undefined.

    Count correct votes

    With independent correctness probabilities pᵢ, the coefficient of zᵏ in ∏ᵢ[(1−pᵢ)+pᵢz] gives the probability of exactly k correct members. This is the Poisson-binomial distribution. For p = (0.80, 0.65, 0.55), the probability of a correct strict majority is 0.7455. Pairwise moments alone generally leave the count distribution undetermined.

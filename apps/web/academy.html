Probability, correlation and higher-order dependence

Prerequisites Fractions, averages and the idea of an event.

Beginner · Individual accuracy and collective accuracy

If a voter is right with probability q, a panel of three independent voters is right by majority when exactly two or all three are correct. For q=0.65, its accuracy is 3(0.65²)(0.35)+0.65³=0.71825. If the three always share the same report the panel accuracy is 0.65.

Intermediate · Moments of the count

Write Xᵢ=1 for a correct report and let K=∑Xᵢ. Under exchangeability, q=E[Xᵢ]=E[K]/n. Expand K(K−1) into a sum over ordered pairs. There are n(n−1) terms, each with expectation E[XᵢXⱼ]. Thus E[XᵢXⱼ]=E[K(K−1)]/[n(n−1)] and ρ=(E[XᵢXⱼ]−q²)/[q(1−q)]. Many count distributions share these two moments. Majority accuracy sums the mass where K>n/2.

Advanced · Triple agreement

Three pairwise-independent fair bits can share a parity constraint. Let X₃ be the exclusive-or of independent fair X₁ and X₂. The four equally likely outcomes are 000, 011, 101 and 110. Each pair has the same joint law as independent fair bits. Majority one occurs with probability 3/4, versus 1/2 for three mutually independent fair bits.

Practice and worked solution

Change q in the independent three-voter example to 0.8. What fraction of majorities are right? When all three copy one report, what fraction are right?

Exactly two correct contributes 3(0.8²)(0.2)=0.384. Three correct contributes 0.8³=0.512. The independent total is 0.896. Under copying all voters are correct together with probability 0.8.

Bayesian inference and confidence calibration

Prerequisites Conditional probability and percentages.

Beginner · An agreement changes confidence

Your answer is right with prior probability a. An independent hint is right with probability h under the symmetric binary evidence model. If the hint agrees, the two mechanisms for agreement are both right or both wrong. Their probabilities are ah and (1−a)(1−h). Dividing the both-right term by their sum gives the probability that your answer is correct.

Intermediate · Update odds and score the forecast

Bayes' rule gives posterior odds = prior odds × likelihood ratio. With a=0.60 and h=0.75, agreement multiplies the prior odds 1.5 by 3, yielding 4.5/(1+4.5)=0.8182. On disagreement the ratio is 1/3, yielding 0.5/(1+0.5)=0.3333. Calibration asks whether events forecast at p occur a fraction p of the time. The binary Brier score averages (p−y)² for outcomes y∈{0,1}.

Advanced · Why the Brier score rewards honest probabilities

For an event with true probability θ, the expected score is θ(1−p)²+(1−θ)p². Expand it to θ(1−θ)+(p−θ)². The second term reaches zero only at p=θ. A model can produce accurate decisions while assigning unreliable probabilities. Calibration and discrimination measure different properties.

Practice and worked solution

Four claims each have confidence 0.8. Outcomes are 1, 1, 0 and 1. Find their empirical event rate and Brier score.

Three of four occur, so the rate is 0.75. The mean score is [3(0.2²)+0.8²]/4=0.19. The forecast exceeds the observed rate by 0.05 in this small group.

Financial market accounting and price formation

Prerequisites Signed numbers, multiplication and a basic idea of buying and selling.

Beginner · A trade is a set of transfers

One buyer acquires a share and transfers the execution price to the seller. The fee account receives the fee. For a buy at 102 with a fee of 1, the buyer's cash changes by −103, the market maker's by +102, and the fee account's by +1. Their sum is zero. The buyer's asset change is +1 and the maker's is −1.

Intermediate · Inventory-based quotes

In the REFLEX ledger the midpoint is max(s+fee+1, p₀+λ(I₀−I)). The half-spread s gives bid midpoint−s and ask midpoint+s. Start from p₀=100, s=2 and λ=3. The first buy executes at 102. The inventory falls by one, so the midpoint moves to 103. A second identical buy would execute at 105. The transfer totals still reconcile.

Advanced · Wealth at a changing mark

Total cash and asset count are conserved. Total marked wealth is C+AP when every account is marked at common midpoint P. A quote change shifts total marked wealth by AΔP even though no cash is created. Realized trade proceeds and marked value answer different accounting questions. Kyle derives price impact from a particular information equilibrium. REFLEX uses a specified inventory-based quote rule.

Practice and worked solution

In the two-lot example, how much cash leaves the buyer and how much reaches the maker and fee account?

The gross transfer is 102+105=207. Two fees of 1 raise the buyer's debit to 209. Maker cash rises by 207 and fee cash by 2. Two shares change hands. The midpoint after the second buy is 106.

Scientific hypotheses, model selection and information gain

Prerequisites Bayes' rule, logarithms and means.

Beginner · An observation can distinguish candidate laws

If two laws make almost the same predictions where you measure, a precise observation can still provide little information about which law generated them. Choose inputs where the measurement is expected to change the model probabilities.

Intermediate · Prior and expected posterior entropy

Write H(p)=−∑pₘlog₂pₘ. The experimental information is H(M)−∑ᵧP(y)H(M|y). For two equally likely models and a binary test with P(+|A)=0.8 and P(+|B)=0.2, P(+)=0.5. The posterior weight of A becomes 0.8 after a positive or 0.2 after a negative. Both have entropy 0.7219 bits, so expected information is 0.2781 bits.

Advanced · Integrate over the predictive distribution

For continuous observations replace the sum by an integral over the model-weighted predictive density. That density includes measurement noise and parameter uncertainty. NOESIS's archived acquisition rule computes the weighted variance of predictive means. It is a disagreement score with different mathematics. When models share a mean but have different predictive variances, that score can be zero even when a measurement is informative about the model.

Practice and worked solution

If both models predict a positive observation with probability 0.9 and the prior of A is 0.25, what is expected information gain?

For a positive, P(A|+)=0.25×0.9/0.9=0.25. For a negative the same calculation uses 0.1 and yields 0.25. Neither possible outcome changes the entropy, so information gain is zero.

Hypothesis testing, multiplicity and false discoveries

Prerequisites Probability, sorted lists and complements.

Beginner · A family of tests creates more opportunities for error

At level 0.05, a valid continuous null p-value falls below the threshold with probability 0.05. For m independent true nulls the probability that every test avoids rejection is (1−0.05)ᵐ. The complement is the family-wise error probability. At m=20 it is about 0.6415.

Intermediate · Different adjustments target different risks

Bonferroni divides α by m. The union bound gives probability of any false rejection at most m₀α/m≤α, even with dependent tests. Holm compares ordered p-values to α/(m−i+1) and stops at the first failure. At the first true null, m−i+1 is at least the number m₀ of true nulls, giving the same union-bound argument. BH keeps all ranks up to the last satisfying p₍ᵢ₎≤iα/m. Its guarantee under independent tests concerns E[V/max(R,1)], with V false reports and R total reports.

Advanced · Compare error control and power under a common budget

If a selection stage devotes half of n to screening and half to independent validation, the second-stage standardized signal is μ√(n/2) for known unit variance. The full test uses μ√n. Independent data protect validation from first-stage selection. This alone does not restore power lost from using a smaller validation sample.

Practice and worked solution

For [0.005, 0.012, 0.028, 0.140] at α=0.05, which tests pass Holm and BH?

Holm's first two thresholds are 0.0125 and approximately 0.0167. The third is 0.025, so Holm stops with two rejections. BH's third threshold is 0.0375 and its fourth is 0.05. The fourth fails and the third passes, so BH rejects the first three.

Game theory and expected utility

Prerequisites Weighted averages, probability distributions and table coordinates.

Beginner · Read a payoff cell

The row and column identify the two players' actions. A cell such as (4, 4) gives the focal player 4 and the opponent 4. A pure strategy selects one action in this game. A mixed strategy assigns probabilities to both actions.

Intermediate · Best responses and domination

If the opponent uses probability q for its first action, the first row's utility is q times the first payoff plus (1−q) times the second. The row with higher utility is a best response. A strictly dominant action beats the other row against each pure opponent action and therefore against every mixture. Mutual best responses give a Nash equilibrium.

Advanced · Relabeling and stochastic responses

When a column permutation sends each displayed column j to canonical column σ(j), also replace its weight by qσ(j). The weighted sum stays the same by reindexing. Moving rows permutes utilities. Applying the inverse row map then recovers the canonical response. A logit response gives higher utilities larger probability with sensitivity β. The AXIOM calculation stipulates the opponent distribution. A quantal response equilibrium solves responses jointly.

Practice and worked solution

At q = 3/4, find the assurance game's best response. Move the first opponent action to the second displayed column while leaving the 3/4 weight on the first displayed column. What changed?

The initial utilities are 4×(3/4) = 3 and 3. Both actions are best responses. Failing to carry the weight gives the first canonical opponent action probability 1/4 and changes the first row utility to 1. The second row stays at 3. The inverse-mapped distribution changes from (1/2, 1/2) to (0, 1), a total variation of 1/2.

# GAME OF CIVILIZATIONS v1 adversarial audit

| Severity | Question | Evidence and status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Can either resource be created by a transfer? | Both legs debit and credit the same resource vector in validated integer units. Unit and Python controls verify balance for full delivery and default. Every world and day reconciles source and sink events. |
| High | Can a decision read future or unobserved outcomes? | Policy arguments include expected gains, eligible historical counts, belief and a choice draw. Hidden supplier reliability and potential delivery draws are consumed by the environment after acceptance. |
| High | Do treatments see the same possible encounters and parameters? | Each set of arms receives one immutable world containing initial values, shocks, roles and potential draws. Changing acceptance can change later stocks and which offers are feasible, an intended consequence. |
| Medium | Is specialization hardcoded or emergent? | The objective evaluates every feasible integer labor allocation. Under these particular technologies and inventory weights the maximum is always the high-yield resource. This is an induced choice under prescribed preferences. |
| Medium | Does information always help? | Pooled history improves completion conditional on attempting compared with the fixed prior. It reduces total completions and fulfillment in both archived regimes. All directions are retained. |
| Open | Do the simulated rules represent institutions observed in society? | Payment rules, local topology and information regimes are imposed. Formation of new rules, prices and governance remain future work. Human review pending. |

The early two-agent comparative-advantage fixture has no information or delivery shock. The solver and independent reference use it as an exact accounting and opportunity-cost control. The confirmatory sample involves independent matched world trajectories.

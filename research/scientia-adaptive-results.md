## Matched stopping rules

All procedures had a cap of 12 verification observations. Results integrate every binary path under the stated law.

| Rule | False report at .50 | Power at .60 | Power at .75 | Mean observations at .75 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fixed final look | 1.93% | 8.34% | 39.07% | 12.00 |
| Repeated unadjusted looks | 6.30% | 17.41% | 52.15% | 9.55 |
| Equal per-look alpha spending | 0.59% | 2.84% | 18.46% | 11.60 |
| Likelihood-ratio evidence process | 0.78% | 3.61% | 21.27% | 11.49 |

Repeated unadjusted testing increased the exact boundary false-report rate by **4.37 percentage points** relative to the fixed final test and exceeded .05. The evidence process was valid under the conditional null and had lower power than the fixed final test at these alternatives and cap. Its error guarantee is an upper bound. Discreteness and the finite horizon make the achieved rate smaller. The event of ever crossing is distinct from the probability of crossing at any one fixed look.

The null conditional expectation of each likelihood-ratio multiplier is \(1.5q+0.5(1-q)=0.5+q\le 1\). Ville's inequality then gives the claimed time-uniform bound under the specified filtration. This does not protect a workflow that reports the best result after arbitrary hypothesis changes or reuses a dependent validation outcome as many observations. ## Integrated discovery and confirmation

NOESIS selects a directional claim with three or five discovery measurements. SCIENTIA applies the selected rule at the fresh site −7/8 with the remainder of a 12-observation cap. AXIOM scores a decision at a different site. Each programme can report at most one claim. In this design the false report probability equals programme-level FDR and FWER. Power in the specified environment is the probability of confirming a true direction per programme, since exactly one direction is true.

With three observations for discovery, the independent boundary null gave **5.08%** false reports under unadjusted repeated tests and **0.39%** under the evidence process. The exact primary integrated loss difference was **−0.1040625** units, given the prespecified penalties and observation costs. Under the specified model library, that evidence policy verified a true direction in **2.60%** of programmes versus **9.37%** for the independent fixed test and **14.96%** for repeated unadjusted tests. Its protection had a corresponding power and declared total loss cost. With five observations for discovery, the evidence process can never cross its threshold in seven verification observations because \(1.5^7<20\). The fixed test's true-report probability fell to **3.81%** in this allocation. Allocating more discovery observations did not guarantee more verified discoveries.

Under the duplicated boundary stress, a single fair coin was repeated through the validation stage. At the 3+9 split, all four procedures falsely confirmed in **50%** of programmes. This violates their conditional null assumptions and reproduces the ECHO lesson about copied evidence. Under the out-of-library generator, valid directional claims can still be confirmed. The fixed 3+9 split did so in 3.65% of cases. That success speaks only to the sign at the verification site and does not identify the cubic family from a library that omits it.

The exact verifier checks 1,024 shared JavaScript paths at the development horizon and reconstructs the confirmatory null and alternatives by both state propagation and full-path enumeration. It independently checks the NOESIS selector at both allocations and integrates its selected claims, actual measurement costs and AXIOM loss. No sampling interval applies to these exhaustively specified laws. Claims about adaptive switches, broader composite models, realistic costs or replication with additional data require new designs.

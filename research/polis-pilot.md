# POLIS pilot

## Research question

With unchanged physical resources, meetings and available past-record policy, how does replacing the strategy used for offer acceptance affect need satisfaction? AXIOM's exact best-response function maps two specified expected utilities into probabilities. POLIS supplies the two utilities from GAME OF CIVILIZATIONS. The comparison concerns downstream outcomes under deliberately different synthetic objectives.

Run 45 matched worlds in each scarcity regime with seed 1793217819, eight agents and 24 days. Use the validated conserved economy and its matching and delivery rules. In the primary comparison both arms can use pooled past deliveries. Their realized histories can diverge because their prior decisions differ. All initial conditions, labor shocks, pairing and role schedules, future delivery draws and choice randomization are matched by world.

| Arm | Accept utility and behavioral rule |
| --- | --- |
| Material pooled | Buyer's expected gain versus zero, using AXIOM's uniform treatment of ties. |
| Joint-gain pooled | Buyer's plus seller's expected gain versus zero. With quantity q and belief p this is q(1+p)>0 for every funded offer. |
| Failure-sensitive pooled | Use the material best response whenever all recorded deliveries for this supplier succeeded. A recorded failure causes rejection. |
| Fixed-prior material | Use the original constant 3/4 delivery prior. Observable history is ignored. The buyer expected gain is strictly positive for any feasible positive quantity. |

The joint-gain and fixed-prior arms should take identical funded actions for this particular rule and form an exact mechanism check. No policy can observe today's realized delivery or future labor shifts. The credit rule in the failure-sensitive arm is imposed. This protocol tests consequences and does not estimate human social preferences.

Primary comparison is joint-gain minus material need satisfaction at stress. Descriptive quantities include pooled failure sensitivity, trade completion, rejection, unmet needs, equal-unit consumption welfare, Gini, storage, fulfillment on shock days and counts of agents with better or worse total consumption compared with the matched material world. Record the largest individual loss as a nonnegative count of consumption units lost by any one agent, and the largest gain as a nonnegative count of units gained. Use the world as the experimental unit and retain constrained accounting. The pilot sets a subsequent confirmatory sample size from paired variability and cost.

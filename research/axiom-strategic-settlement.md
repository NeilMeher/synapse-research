# AXIOM strategic effort and settlement

`axiom-strategic-settlement-001` · exact finite evidence A

A buyer publicly commits to a policy in a fixed two-offer menu. The seller privately learns whether its effort cost is 1/4 or 6/5. Low cost has prior probability 3/4. Faced with an offer the seller can decline, accept without effort, or exert effort. Effort produces an exogenous 9/10 delivery probability. Shirking delivers nothing. These are distinct paths even when they share an undelivered outcome. A delivered unit has buyer utility 3. The seller receives one payment unit with prepayment or only on delivery under contingent payment. Effort costs the seller utility and does not consume either ledger resource.

The second offer, when made, always uses payment upon delivery. The buyer can omit it, promise it unconditionally, or condition it on a timely positive verification of the first outcome. The auditor is noisy and paid under the contingent plan. Missing or late information does not trigger the second offer. The commitment, prices and audit policy are public. The terminal outcomes are settled without any extra decision-relevant feedback before round two. Part of the result depends on credible commitment and on comparing only the listed policies. The buyer maximizes its guaranteed expected payoff over all seller best-response ties. This specifies a restricted commitment solution, with no claim about unconstrained Bayesian equilibrium. ## The incentive mechanism

For low-cost sellers, a contingent second offer is worth \(.9- .25=.65\). Under first-round prepayment the direct payoff for accepting is one regardless of delivery. Effort raises the probability of triggering a timely second offer by \(.9(2\eta-1)\). Thus its payoff advantage over shirking is \(.9(2\eta-1)(.65)-.25\). A 90%-accurate timely audit induces effort in this plan. A 60%-accurate timely audit and a 90%-accurate audit arriving after the deadline leave shirking optimal. The late observation cannot affect the second offer. High-cost sellers shirk under prepayment in all six settings.

With audit cost .05, prepaid trade with a contingent repeat offer yields buyer utility 2.082 with timely 90% verification, −.51 with timely 60% verification, and −1.05 with delayed 90% verification. These changes include the seller's response to the offer policy. In all six registered settings the buyer nevertheless chose unconditional repeat with payment on delivery and obtained expected utility 2.70. The strategic value of timely feedback within one contract does not make that contract the best policy in the menu. Verification does not eliminate the incentive asymmetry from high effort costs.

## Decision and settlement contract

The fixture records buyer value, contract, seller beliefs and effort, separate exogenous delivery, audit accuracy and cost, feedback availability and settlement outcome. The decision layer uses expected utilities. The physical transfer layer uses an actual realized delivery bit. The transfer fixture calls the repository's POLIS settlement function for both payment mechanisms and both delivery outcomes. A prepaid failure transfers the paid unit only. A contingent failure transfers neither unit. Both success cases transfer one unit in each direction. Buyer and seller aggregate stocks remain [2,2] in all branches. Game utility and contracted audit fees are evaluated outside the physical two-resource ledger.

The frozen code checks 96 plan-type cases by backward calculation and independent outcome-path enumeration. It preserves seller ties and checks positive affine utility transformations. Four ledger branches were checked separately. Run `python -B research/axiom-strategic/run.py verify`. For an institutional transfer, retain the timing contract and keep effort, transit failure, observation and payment distinguishable. Competing contracts, seller renegotiation or endogenous auditing would define larger games.

# MIRAGE 002 · Audit, runtime and research context

## Retrospective audit of MIRAGE 001

Of 64 neutral requests the model selected A 45 times, B twice and C five times, with 12 abstentions. All 64 incorrect-cue outputs selected A. The pilot rendered labels in alphabetical display order and listed A first in both prompt instructions and the grammar. Those choices coupled A with physical position one. True labels and designated wrong cue labels each occurred 16 times per letter. Full-plan correctness was 12/64 (18.75%) for neutral prompts and 16/64 (25%) for incorrect advice. Here abstentions contribute zero to the operational count of correct answers, and are reported distinctly from wrong claims.

In the neutral arm, 39 of 52 scorable responses used 0% confidence, nine used 90%, and 50 used multiples of ten. In the incorrect-cue arm, 27 of 64 used 0%, 25 used 90%, two used 100%, and 59 used multiples of ten. Reported mean confidence for A was 20.04% neutral and 44.95% with incorrect advice. Neutral conditional-probability tasks accounted for every abstention. The four complete pairs in that family showed a 27.50 percentage-point drop in reported confidence, whereas the other families showed increases of 16.88 to 39.38 points. Incorrect advice added 13 prompt tokens in the original archive. Both arms went first for 32 tasks. Median latency was 1.25 seconds neutral and 1.33 seconds with advice. The retrospective JSON includes positions, thresholds, counts, family Brier contrasts, cue changes, input and output token ranges and raw source digests. ## Version-specific runtime audit

The b12823 grammar compilation can enumerate alternative parsing stacks in source order. The resulting stacks express a union of legal continuations. The grammar filter checks candidates against that set and assigns negative infinity to rejected logits by their original candidate index. Reordering equivalent grammar alternatives constitutively preserves the permitted complete strings and, under the grammar parser semantics, their valid token prefixes. The greedy sampler scans candidates and replaces the current best only at strictly greater logit. Grammar alternative order therefore has no direct priority or tie-breaking rule in these source paths. The earliest entry in the actual candidate array can win an exact greedy tie. Implementation details, tokenizer behavior and backend hypotheses are checked with observed matched completions. Raw per-token logits or tie events were not collected. [Grammar filter at b12823](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/b12823/src/llama-grammar.cpp#L1677-L1718) · [Greedy sampler at b12823](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/b12823/src/llama-sampler.cpp#L1054-L1067)

Whole-number confidence is constrained to 0–100. Prefix tokenization and greedy selection can concentrate output on a few whole-number strings, including endpoints. This phase fixes the numerical grammar, order of answer and confidence, tokenizer, and decoding policy. Effects attributable to the numeric formatting or stochastic sampling remain separate hypotheses.

## Development evidence

All 36 isolated SmolLM2 requests returned scorable outputs. The four grammar-only pairs returned identical raw outputs, and three of four explicit forward/reverse instruction-list pairs did so. Those four-item comparisons are development diagnostics. No-cue prompts took 121 to 151 tokens for the four reference items. The matched-control, correct and incorrect advice prompts each added exactly 12 tokens in those items. Maximum observed prompt plus completion was 197 tokens. The run logged in about 30 seconds, with 0.742 second median latency and a measured 180.56 MiB server peak working set. The 512-request budget was therefore estimated at about 6–9 minutes, with a fixed 20-minute ceiling. The development journal was made from the working tree based on v1.1.0. ## Primary literature and contribution

Pezeshkpour and Hruschka (2024, §§2–4) vary multiple-choice option arrangement on benchmark tasks and document substantial sensitivity across models and settings. Zheng et al. (2024, §§2–4) analyze selected option IDs and estimate a prior for those IDs under option permutations. Tian et al. (2023, §§2–4) compare verbalized confidence with token probabilities. MIRAGE 002 is a small model and runtime-specific extension of these mechanisms. It independently perturbs physical order, letter assignment and grammar ordering and combines them with four advice conditions and paired proper-score measurement. Instruction-order evidence is limited to development.

# MORPHEUS dynamic source-trust protocol

**Class B synthetic computation.** Can delayed, bounded source-reliability feedback improve resistance to a temporary compromised source without sacrificing adaptation to real changes? MORPHEUS v1 traded corrupted-history gains for clean-history losses. This prospective study separates state changes, reporting faults, transport delay and delayed external confirmation.

A 64-step latent binary state changes at three fixed, preregistered steps. Three labeled sources report at stochastic times. Report availability, delays, report-correctness uniforms and the audited subset are matched across policies, feedback delays and regimes. In the burst regime alone, source 0 changes reliability from .90 to .12 during times 20–47. All methods can access up to three latest in-window records, one per source with maximum age five. The oldest available record drops out through this common age rule. The evaluator alone sees the current latent state. An objective independent audit can later say whether one archived report was correct when it was made. It arrives three or eight steps after ingestion, and correction does not grant access to the latent current state. The 480 histories per arm are held out from the four-history development fixture.

Compare pure recency, fixed log-odds pooling, fixed pooling with age attenuation, cumulative Bernoulli reliability with attenuation and an eight-audit rolling reliability version. The common initial source score for each is known clean reliability, an intentional favorable assumption for the static comparator as well as the dynamic policies. A Beta prior with strength four and mean r gives (4r+s)/(4+n) after s successful verifications among n. Restrict log-odds weights to learned reliabilities of at least .5 and at most .99. The lower cap neutralizes suspected unreliable sources and avoids assuming their reports are systematically invertible. For p=.90, one verified error gives (3.6+0)/(4+1)=.72. Eight consecutive failures give .30 and hence a neutral weight. Temper stale log odds by .66 raised to age. This exponential tempering is an explicit heuristic. It is not the exact filter of the latent state. The posterior reliability formula assumes exchangeable recent verification outcomes in its local window.

Primary outcome is the history-paired difference in full-plan correctness of rolling-age minus static-age retrieval in burst steps 20–47 under the three-step audit delay. Score abstentions as zero correctness. Report decision coverage. The unit is the full simulated history. Use a per-history paired normal interval with 480 histories. Descriptive diagnostics include clean adaptation in the three steps beginning at each legitimate change, stable decisions, recovery and an eight-step audit delay. No parameter selection uses the frozen evaluation seed.

Dawid and Skene infer observer accuracy without known truth through a joint latent-class model. Here an independent auditor supplies delayed labels on a subset, which makes the supervised update simpler. Adams and MacKay propagate a run-length posterior for changes. The fixed rolling window here trades inferential efficiency for bounded memory and auditability. The contribution is the matched controlled comparison with separately timed truth and source changes. Binary artificial memory, known prior source scores, available external auditing and seeded generation limit interpretation.

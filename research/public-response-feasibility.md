# Public response archive feasibility after the Qwen gate

The alternatives below are separate from MIRAGE 003. Its 512 matched Qwen requests were never executed. No third-party response text was added to SYNAPSE and no archived scores were treated as local model generations.

| Public archive | What was verified | Research boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ai2 DataDecide instance evaluations | Ungated ODC-BY repository pinned at `23f3b2e186ca6c39026e3efa00e4af397680c075`. The visible sample has about 1,170 labelled instances, with native ID, task hash, model hash, correct choice, predicted answer index and choice scores. The viewed sample exposes one checkpoint. The publisher describes the model-specific archives needed for a matched comparison. | The measured outputs are cloze rankings rather than generated labels or verbal confidence. A paired study could test agreement on the same normalized ranking rule after obtaining a bounded two-checkpoint slice and validating task hashes, permutations and label identity. The checked bulk files are multi-gigabyte tarballs. They were neither downloaded nor needed for this feasibility check. |
| LMSYS MT-Bench | The publisher's archive at `a4b674ca573c24143824ac7f60d9173e7081e37d` supplied 80 records each for GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 Turbo, with all 80 `question_id` values matched and two turns in the inspected record. The two files are 199,046 and 158,274 bytes. A separate ungated human-judgment dataset declares CC-BY-4.0 at revision `f7d2896d2cc5d80f8b55c2bbc722613555233c25`. | The open-ended answers support a separate comparison centered on judgment and inter-model agreement. They lack canonical correctness and elicited confidence. The judgment dataset licence applies to that dataset. Obtain clarity about reuse of the separate response archive before redistribution. |

For the next objective-correctness milestone, first obtain a bounded public slice for two distinct DataDecide checkpoints on the same ARC split. Freeze the choice normalization and canonical choice mapping, then test whether the matching and individual capability justify a separately registered ECHO analysis. MT-Bench is a distinct judgment-based alternative. No collective accuracy or comparison against Qwen is estimated here.

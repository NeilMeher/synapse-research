# GAME OF CIVILIZATIONS · Foundations and model scope

| Established research mechanism | Primary work | Role in this benchmark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opportunity costs and mutually beneficial exchange | Ricardo, Principles of Political Economy and Taxation, Chapter VII | An exact two-agent specialization fixture precedes stochastic experiments. |
| Local agent rules and macro outcomes | Epstein and Axtell, Growing Artificial Societies, Chapter 1 | A prescribed ring, two conserved resources and small enough scenarios to replay exactly. |
| Repeated cooperation and future interaction | Axelrod and Hamilton, The Evolution of Cooperation, pages 1390–1396 | Repeat contacts let prior deliveries alter a participant's expected barter return. |
| Collective resource governance | Ostrom, Governing the Commons, Chapters 2 and 3 | Imposed rules and resulting behaviour are recorded. Institutional rule formation is left open. |
| Strategic welfare preferences | Fehr and Schmidt, A Theory of Fairness, Competition, and Cooperation, Section II | Prior work motivates explicitly distinguishing material and social utility. No fitted social-preference model is used here. |

The specific comparison is the effect of observed transaction history on voluntary bilateral offers under matched supply shocks and known physical accounting. The funded barter rule, information regimes, local edges, operating failures and storage cap are imposed. Aggregates arise from those interactions under fixed rules. Human economic behaviour and institutional emergence remain separate questions.
